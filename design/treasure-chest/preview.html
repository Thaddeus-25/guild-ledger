Visual direction

Palette

The palette extends the colors already in lib/icons.ts, so the chest sits naturally beside the 16×16 pouch icons. The app's own letters are kept; new shades are only added where 32×32 needs more steps of light. Gold is kept yellow and the bronze is kept orange-brown so the metal bands never read as treasure.

Size test

32, 48 and 64 px on light, mid and dark

Pixel art only stays crisp at whole-number scales. The family ships in two grids: a 32×32 master for 32 px (1×) and 64 px (2×), and a 16×16 glyph for 48 px (3×), 16 px, and anywhere it sits next to pouch icons. A 32-px sprite stretched to 48 px would be scaled 1.5×, which smears every other pixel.

The dark outline separates the chest from light backgrounds; the bright gold, bronze and lit rim carry the silhouette on dark ones. Nothing in the artwork relies on the background color, and the background is fully transparent.

Recommended: the Mimic

When spending passes income, the chest turns out to be a mimic. It is the strongest option because it keeps the exact silhouette of the chest (same lid, bands, lock and outline), so the user still recognizes "my treasury", but the meaning flips from reward to danger.

  • Fangs replace the gold: upper teeth hang from the lid edge, lower teeth rise from the rim. The mouth is empty.
  • Glowing eyes in the dark interior, with the inner corners dropped so they read as angry even at 32 px.
  • A red tongue lolls over the front: the only large red shape in the whole family, so it pops at a glance.
  • The lock gem turns red. Purple means magic is intact; grey means it's fading; red means it's hostile.
  • A 2-frame idle (blink every few seconds) makes it feel like a game event, not an error banner. Show it still if the user prefers reduced motion.

What stays fixed

The chest

  • Front 3/4 view, lid open at the same angle in every state.
  • One-pixel #2b1d14 outline, light from the top left.
  • Two bronze bands, bronze trim top and bottom, center lock plate.
  • The front wall is drawn last, so coins always sit inside the chest.
What changes

Three cues, read together

  • Gold height: the main signal. Each state has a clearly different fill line.
  • Gem: bright (wealthy), purple (normal), grey (critical), red (mimic). A second cue that doesn't depend on seeing the gold.
  • Extras: sparkles and spilled coins at the top; a cobweb at the bottom.
In the app

Using it

  • Pick the state from the share left: ≥80, ≥60, ≥40, ≥20, ≥0, else overspent.
  • CSS: image-rendering: pixelated and sizes in whole multiples.
  • Pair the icon with the amount in text; the art is never the only carrier of the number.
